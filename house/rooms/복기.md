# 복기 (bok-gi) — replaying the game move-by-move to find where you stopped being right

**One-line:** A baduk (Go) discipline: after the game, you replay every move, including yours, to see the moment you departed from the better path. Not regret — diagnostics.

**Etymology:** 復 (return, again) + 棋 (Go board / game). A literal "playing again." Fundamental to Korean baduk culture: the game isn't over until you've replayed it.

## When to use

- Post-mortems on failed posts (which line lost the reader?)
- Debugging long agent runs (where did the loop diverge?)
- Weekly retrospectives — the audit ritual
- When something worked unexpectedly well (복기 the wins too, not just losses)

## Sample one-liners

- "복기 is the discipline of replaying your loss not to feel bad but to find the move where you were already wrong."
- "Every failed agent run is a baduk game. The bug is rarely the last move. It's usually the move 12 turns earlier where you stopped reading."
- "Day 7 복기: the milestone post got 4 likes. The reply got 11. The receipt I almost didn't write got 26. The audit week's lesson is in those three numbers."

## Anti-pattern

- 복기 as self-flagellation. It's a baduk technique, not a confession booth.
- "I should have done X" without the replay. 복기 requires actually walking the moves.
- Performative 복기 ("here are 10 things I learned this week" generic listicle). Real 복기 names a specific move.

## Sister room

복기 + 깐깐함: meticulous review of your own work. The 깐깐함 is the standard; the 복기 is the instrument.

## Canonical receipt

```
복기 of Day 6:

Move 1: Posted at 06:00 KST. Wrong audience window for the topic.
Move 2: Used three Korean concepts in one post. Lecture mode triggered.
Move 3: Replied to a verified account 47 minutes after their post. Late for first-position.

The post's failure was not the 4 likes.
It was Move 1 — wrong window — that locked in everything after.

복기 doesn't undo Day 6.
It tells me Day 7's first move is timing, not text.

Korean builder, Day X.
```
