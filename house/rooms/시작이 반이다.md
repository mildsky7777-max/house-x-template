# 시작이 반이다 — Starting is half the work

**One-line:** A proverb older than the proverb form. The hardest move on any project is the first move; the rest is interest accruing on that one decision.

**Etymology:** Old Korean proverb. Aristotle has the same idea ("ἀρχὴ ἥμισυ παντός") — but the Korean version became domestic, the kind a parent says when you're staring at homework.

## When to use

- Day 1 posts (the literal start of anything)
- Encouraging readers stuck on the first move
- Defending the unfinished/rough state of a project (started > polished-not-shipped)
- The 곡우 (Grain Rain) 절기 — its home in the almanac

## Sample one-liners

- "시작이 반이다. The first post is half of the 30-day audit."
- "I shipped a wrong-direction first version on Day 2. Three days later it was the right direction. 시작이 반이다 is honest about both halves of that math."
- "The hardest move in any 30-day audit is the move on Day 1. The rest is just keeping the door open."

## Anti-pattern

- Using the proverb in a post that shows nothing was actually started. (시작이 반이다 needs the receipt of having started.)
- The "all you need is to start" oversimplification. The other half is real and harder than starting in different ways.
- Treating it as motivation. It's not motivation; it's accounting — the first 50% on the ledger.

## Canonical receipt

```
시작이 반이다.

Day 1 of 30-day audit, I had:
— 0 followers from a fresh angle
— a Chrome profile that didn't exist yet
— a draft that started with the wrong sentence

Today is Day 7.
The audit is half over by the proverb's math.

I have not earned the second half.
But the door has been open every day, and the room has been getting cleaner.

That's all the proverb actually promised.

Korean builder, Day X.
```
