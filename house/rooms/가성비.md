# 가성비 (ga-seong-bi) — price-to-performance as a moral stance

**One-line:** What it actually does ÷ what it actually costs. Not "cheap." Not "value." A measurable ratio that makes you suspicious of inflated price tags and skeptical of free things that aren't.

**Etymology:** 가격(price) + 성능(performance) + 비(ratio). Coined by ordinary Koreans, not marketers. The street invented the word; the corporations had to learn it.

## When to use

- LLM model comparison ("$3/M vs $15/M for what gain?")
- Tool stack defense ("we run on a $5/mo VPS not a $200/mo platform")
- Subscription audits ("the 10x feature the 10x price doesn't deliver")
- Refusing the sponsorship-shaped trap ("the watch isn't 가성비; it's just expensive")

## Sample one-liners

- "가성비 is not 'cheap.' It's the audit of every dollar against what that dollar actually moves."
- "I run my X auto-publish stack on a free local server and a free Chrome profile. The Premium check is the only thing I pay X for."
- "Sonnet for drafts. Opus for the moment that decides the post. 가성비 is knowing which model to call when."

## Anti-pattern

- Using 가성비 to justify something that's just cheap and bad. (가성비 demands a real performance number.)
- Using it to dunk on premium tools without showing the math. (Korean street kids do not respect lazy 가성비 takes.)
- "Insanely good 가성비" — caps + the word "insane" → AI-tell.

## Canonical receipt

```
The Sonnet/Opus split is a 가성비 question, not a snobbery question.

Sonnet 3.5 writes 80% of my X drafts.
Opus writes the post that decides the week.

That ratio (8 drafts to 1) makes my month bill ≈ $12.
It also makes my hit rate twice what pure-Opus runs cost in March.

Cheap is not 가성비.
Expensive is not 가성비 either.
Knowing the moment is 가성비.

Korean builder, Day X.
```
