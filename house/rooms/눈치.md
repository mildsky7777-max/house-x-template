# 눈치 (nun-chi) — reading the room before words

**One-line:** The continuous, ambient calculation of what the people around you actually need, before anyone says anything. Korean kids learn it before they learn to read.

**Etymology:** 눈 (eye) + 치 (measure / gauge). Literally "eye-measuring." The body tells you what the conversation is about before the conversation starts.

## When to use

- Reply timing (the moment when the person actually wants to be replied to, not earlier)
- Prompt engineering ("the LLM doesn't have 눈치, which is why your prompt has to encode it")
- Knowing when NOT to post a Korean concept (lecture mode = 눈치 failure)
- Recognizing when a post should be silence

## Sample one-liners

- "눈치 is the reason a Korean grandmother knows you're hungry before you do."
- "An agent without 눈치 in the prompt is technically working and emotionally wrong."
- "X has no 눈치. That's why the timeline feels like a room where everyone is talking and nobody is listening."

## Anti-pattern

- Naming 눈치 in a post that itself fails 눈치 (replying with a Korean lecture to someone announcing a death — see Day 6 sama farewell failure).
- 눈치 as self-praise ("I have great 눈치"). The word doesn't survive being claimed; it's only proven by the next move.
- Translating 눈치 as "intuition." Intuition is a private gut feeling. 눈치 is a public, ambient discipline.

## Sister room

눈치 + 정성: the slow attention. The 눈치 reads what's needed; the 정성 brings it.

## Canonical receipt

