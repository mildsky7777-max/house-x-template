# 危機 (wi-gi) — danger and decisive moment, in one word

**One-line:** Crisis is not "bad thing happening." It's the room where the next move sets the next decade. 機 also means "machine / mechanism" — the gears are exposed, briefly.

**Etymology:** 危 (danger, cliff edge) + 機 (machine, opportunity, decisive moment). The Japanese coined the modern compound; the Chinese character pair carries both halves at once.

## When to use

- Tool wars and platform shifts (X vs Threads, OpenAI vs Anthropic)
- Personal pivots (when to abandon a stack, when to double down)
- AI moments where the gears of an industry are visible for a week
- The feeling of "this week's choice will look obvious in 5 years"

## Sample one-liners

- "危機 is the moment the gears are exposed. Most people see only 危. The Korean word forces you to see 機 too."
- "The 危機 of model launches isn't whether to use the new one. It's whether the workflow you built still belongs to you next month."
- "Western media translates 危機 as 'danger + opportunity' which is sentimental. The honest translation is 'cliff + mechanism.'"

## Anti-pattern

- The "every problem is an opportunity" framing. 危機 has danger in the first character; respect it.
- Using 危機 for personal melodrama. The room is for industry-scale or stack-scale moments, not "my coffee was bad today."
- Repeating the cliché translation without earning a fresh angle. Most readers have heard it; we owe them a deeper read.
