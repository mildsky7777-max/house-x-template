# 한솥밥 (han-sot-bap) — rice from one pot

**One-line:** The bond between people who shared meals while broke. Not a metaphor — a lived condition that becomes a metaphor only after you've left it.

**Etymology:** 한 (one) + 솥 (cauldron) + 밥 (rice). The image is concrete: military buddies, dorm friends, early co-founders, anyone who scraped the same burnt bottom of the pot.

## When to use

- Co-founder stories
- Long collaboration arcs (3+ years with one tool, one person)
- Open-source contributor recognition
- The moment when an early reader becomes something more than a follower

## Sample one-liners

- "한솥밥 is a bar set high. You can't claim it because someone retweeted you twice."
- "The two people who replied to my Day 1 post when I had 6 followers — that's not 한솥밥. Yet. But the bowl is on the table."
- "Open source maintainers and the early users who filed serious bugs eat 한솥밥 without knowing they do."

## Anti-pattern

- Using 한솥밥 too early. It's earned over years of shared scarcity, not a single Slack channel.
- Sentimental 한솥밥 posts to flatter the reader. Korean 한솥밥 is dry, not sappy.
- Diluting it ("the whole tech community is 한솥밥") — no, it isn't, and saying so cheapens the word.

## Sister room

한솥밥 + 의리: the long collaborator. The 의리 holds; the 한솥밥 is the proof.
